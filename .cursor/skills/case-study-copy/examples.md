## Option A

## Analytics in plain English

Most people don't want to learn a BI tool. They just want answers. My team designed an LLM-powered chat experience that sits on top of Dialpad's analytics and responded with interactive charts you can share.

[Main Figure]

The pattern we kept hearing was familiar: the data was already in Dialpad, but people still asked in Slack because opening a dashboard felt like homework. We designed the chat to answer in the same tone as a helpful teammate, then hand back a chart you can paste into the thread instead of another export.

## Option B

## Analytics in plain English

Most people don't want to learn a BI tool. They just want answers. My team designed an LLM-powered chat experience that sits on top of Dialpad's analytics and responded with interactive charts you can share.

[Main Figure]

We weren't trying to replace BI. We were trying to cut the trip from "I have a question" to "here's the chart in the channel." Shareable outputs mattered as much as the model, because the answer only helps if someone else can see it without logging into the same dashboard.

[thumbnail 1]
Hourly heatmap for spotting peaks without building a custom view.

[thumbnail 2]
Stacked series by queue when the story is composition, not a headline number.

[thumbnail 3]
Tabular results for the questions where a chart would just get in the way.
