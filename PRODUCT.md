# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary audience is hiring managers and recruiters evaluating Ted Goas for product design, research, and front-end roles. They arrive with limited time, often between meetings or after a referral link. They need to quickly grasp craft, judgment, and fit: how work is framed, how problems are solved, and whether the person behind the site is someone they would trust on a team.

Secondary visitors (peers, newsletter readers) benefit from the same clarity, but hiring confidence is the success metric.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio and publishing surface for tedgoas.com. It showcases case studies, writing, background, and values so a hiring decision can be informed without a live conversation. Success looks like: a recruiter understands scope and impact from work pages, senses editorial care from typography and layout, and leaves with enough confidence to reach out or advance a candidacy.

The site is not an app. Design is the product: every page is a proof of taste, structure, and restraint.

## Brand Personality

Technical craft, quiet confidence. The voice is direct and expert without performance. Layout and type do the persuading; copy stays factual. The feel is closer to a design engineer's notebook than a marketing funnel: show the work, show how you think, do not sell.

Emotional goal for hiring visitors: assured calm. They should feel they are in capable hands, not being marketed to.

## Anti-references

Explicitly avoid:

- SaaS landing page patterns: hero metrics, identical feature card grids, gradient text, modal-first flows, high-contrast CTA walls
- Generic AI portfolio tropes: Inter/Roboto defaults, purple gradients, decorative glass, side-stripe accent borders, predictable three-column layouts
- Dribbble-shot portfolios where ornament outweighs substance and case studies lack narrative depth
- Corporate marketing sites: stock hero imagery, buzzword headlines, testimonial carousels, "trusted by" logo walls without context

When in doubt, choose editorial long-form over startup landing page.

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't tell** — Case studies and work index carry proof; avoid restating credentials in decorative chrome.
2. **Practice what you preach** — The site itself demonstrates semantic HTML, token-driven CSS, performance-aware choices, and accessible type. The implementation is part of the portfolio.
3. **Quiet hierarchy** — Confidence comes from scale, spacing, and typographic rhythm, not loud color blocks or animation for its own sake.
4. **Respect the reader's time** — Hiring visitors scan first; structure supports skimming (labels, outcomes, clear CTAs) before deep reading.
5. **Content freeze, visual freedom** — Written content is fixed during visual exploration; design changes must not rewrite copy or alter frozen homepage regions documented in the visual design language.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Lean into Atkinson Hyperlegible Next and Mono for body and metadata readability. Maintain sufficient contrast on the forest/teal palette; do not sacrifice legibility for atmosphere. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` for any animation. Semantic HTML and keyboard-accessible navigation are non-negotiable.
